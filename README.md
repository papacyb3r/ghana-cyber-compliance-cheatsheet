# Ghana Cybersecurity and Data Protection: Compliance Cheat Sheet

A quick-reference guide for security and compliance teams operating in Ghana. It summarises the main instruments and who they affect.

> **Not legal advice.** This is a high-level orientation written from public information. Laws, directives and guidance change: always verify against the current official text and the issuing authority before relying on anything here.

## Key instruments

| Instrument | Authority | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Cybersecurity Act, 2020 (Act 1038) | Cyber Security Authority (CSA) | Establishes the CSA; covers critical information infrastructure, licensing/accreditation of cybersecurity service providers and professionals, and incident reporting |
| Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843) | Data Protection Commission | Principles for processing personal data; registration of data controllers; rights of data subjects |
| Bank of Ghana Cyber and Information Security Directive | Bank of Ghana | Cybersecurity requirements for regulated financial institutions |
| Electronic Transactions Act, 2008 (Act 772) | Ministry of Communications | Legal recognition of electronic records and signatures |

## Practical checklist for a regulated organisation

- [ ] Identify whether you operate critical information infrastructure or provide cybersecurity services (accreditation implications)
- [ ] Register with the Data Protection Commission as a data controller where required
- [ ] Maintain a personal data inventory and lawful basis for each processing activity
- [ ] Define incident response and reporting procedures, including regulator notification
- [ ] Align your security programme with a recognised framework (ISO/IEC 27001, NIST CSF)
- [ ] For payments: map PCI DSS requirements alongside regulatory ones
- [ ] Run periodic risk assessments and penetration tests, and keep evidence
